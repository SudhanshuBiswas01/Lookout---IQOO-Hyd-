Covert surveillance in temporary accommodation has transformed into a rampant public safety and human rights issue across India. Micro-cameras are routinely discovered inside:

  • Budget hotel chains, boutique guest rooms, homestays, and Airbnbs
  • Trial rooms and fitting stalls in apparel stores and shopping malls

  • Restrooms, executive washrooms, and gym changing lockers

Victims face severe psychological trauma, extortion, and non-consensual online distribution. Solo female travelers, young couples, and students are disproportionately targeted.

2.2 Why Existing Commercial Solutions Fail

  • Dedicated Bug Detectors (RF Wands): Cost between ₹3,000 and ₹15,000 ($40–$200). They are bulky, require manual tuning, and no traveler carries them in their everyday luggage.
  • Manual Visual Inspections: Modern pinhole lenses measure 1 mm to 2 mm in diameter. Disguised inside screws, smoke detectors, wall clocks, and AC vents, they are virtually invisible to the naked eye under ambient room lighting (>95% miss rate).

2.3 The Fatal Flaw of Existing Mobile Apps

1. The "RF-Only" Blindspot (The SD Card Reality)

Most existing apps only scan local Wi-Fi or Bluetooth networks. In India, over 90% of covert spy cameras record locally onto a hidden MicroSD card. They have no Wi-Fi chips, transmit zero radio frequencies, and are completely invisible to RF scanners.

2. Fake AI & Dangerous Placebos

Dozens of popular Play Store apps display animated green radar graphics with random audio beeps and output arbitrary confidence numbers (e.g., "98% Safe!"). Giving a user a false sense of security is dangerous and irresponsible.

3. The Intolerable Privacy Hazard

Cloud-based "AI camera detector" apps require users to snap photos of their hotel room and upload them to commercial cloud servers for remote processing. Asking a traveler to upload photos of their private bedroom or bathroom destroys the very privacy they seek to protect.

4.1 Principle 1: Optical Retroreflection Physics

The Physical Phenomenon: When light enters a camera lens system, the curved optical glass elements focus incident light onto the focal plane (sensor/aperture). A significant portion of this light bounces off the internal elements and reflects directly back along the entry ray vector.

The Smartphone Geometry Advantage: On modern smartphones, the LED torch is mounted millimeters away from the primary camera sensor (typically 3 mm to 8 mm). This proximity forms an exceptional collinear retroreflective transceiver. Light emitted by the torch strikes a hidden pinhole lens and reflects directly into the phone’s camera sensor.

Angular Envelope Comparison:

  • Retroreflective Lens: Reflects light back to the phone across a broad angular envelope (±15° to ±30°). It remains illuminated as the phone moves.
  • Flat Specular Surface (Screws, Stickers, Polished Tile): Obeys standard specular reflection (θincident = θreflected). It reflects light back to the camera only at one exact orthogonal angle, disappearing instantly upon slight movement.

4.2 Principle 2: Computer Vision Pipeline (blobDetect.js)

To ensure sub-10ms execution and zero battery overheating, Lookout downscales frames to 160 × 120 pixels (19,200 total pixels) and computes using 1D typed arrays.

  1. Luminosity Conversion:
    Y = 0.299 × R + 0.587 × G + 0.114 × B
  2. Adaptive Top-Percentile Thresholding:
    • Calculates threshold targeting the brightest 1.5% pixels (BRIGHT_TOP_FRACTION = 0.015).
    • Enforces an absolute floor of Y ≥ 200/255 (MIN_ABSOLUTE_BRIGHTNESS = 200). In dark scenes, dim sensor noise (Y ≈ 25) is completely ignored.
  3. 8-Connected Component Flood Fill: Iterative queue flood fill identifies contiguous bright regions in a single pass, computing area, bounding box, centroid, and peak brightness.
  4. Four Pinhole Morphological Filters:
    • Area Filter (3 ≤ A ≤ 250 px): Eliminates single-pixel hot noise (<3 px) and ceiling lamps, TV screens, or windows (>250 px).
    • Fill Ratio Filter (≥ 0.5): Area / (Width × Height). A true circular disc has fill π/4 ≈ 0.785. Rejecting <0.5 eliminates diagonal streaks and scratches.
    • Aspect Ratio Filter (≥ 0.5): min(W, H) / max(W, H). Rejects reflections elongated along curtain rods, wires, and shiny edges.
    • Local Contrast Filter (≥ 60): Peak blob brightness must exceed the frame's mean intensity by at least 60 units.

4.3 Principle 3: Multi-Frame Temporal Persistence Tracking (tracker.js)

The core differentiator separating Lookout from all generic detectors.

The Problem: A single still frame cannot differentiate a camera lens from a shiny screw head. Both appear as small, bright, circular spots.
The Solution: Track blob survival across consecutive frames as the phone sweeps through space.
  • Centroid Matching: Matches frame t candidates to frame t-1 tracks using nearest-neighbor Euclidean distance (≤ 14 px).
  • Persistence Accumulation: Matching tracks increment their persistence count: Persistencet = Persistencet-1 + 1.
  • Eviction of Specular Glints: Screws and stickers flash for 1–2 frames and reset. When a track survives ≥ 3 consecutive frames (STRONG_PERSISTENCE_FRAMES = 3), it is promoted to a Strong Track.

4.4 Principle 4: 3D Magnetometer Signal Processing (magnetometer.js)

Unshielded electronics, transformers, microphones, and camera PCB coils create localized perturbations in ambient magnetic fields.

  • Sampling Rate: 10 Hz (every 100 ms) via expo-sensors.
  • 3D Euclidean Magnitude:
    |B| = √(Bx2 + By2 + Bz2) μT
  • Rolling Baseline Buffer: 20-sample circular buffer (2.0s rolling window) establishes room baseline (μbaseline ≈ 35–55 μT) with a 20-sample warmup guard.
  • Spike Detection: Triggers when deviation (|B| - μbaseline) / μbaseline ≥ 0.15 (15% rise above baseline).
  • Sustain & Debounce: Requires 3 consecutive samples over threshold (SPIKE_SUSTAIN_SAMPLES = 3) to filter sensor noise. Debounces with a 2500ms cooldown.
  • Haptic Engine: Vibrations modulate in frequency as magnetic magnitude rises, providing an intuitive metal-detector experience.

4.5 Principle 5: Spatio-Temporal Sensor Fusion (fusion.js)

Standard smartphones do not track absolute spatial coordinates. Lookout uses temporal proximity as a spatial proxy: if a magnetic anomaly spike and an optical glint occur within a 4000 ms window (CORRELATION_WINDOW_MS = 4000) during the same sweep motion, the engine fuses them into a correlated event.

4.6 Principle 6: The Verdict Decision Matrix (verdict.js)

Observed Physical Signal Verdict Copy & Action
Strong Glint (≥ 3 consecutive frames) RED Inspect this spot closely (Lens retroreflection confirmed)
Brief Glint + Correlated Metal Spike RED Inspect this spot closely (Optical & magnetic corroboration)
Brief Glint Alone (1–2 frames) YELLOW Possible lens reflection (Unconfirmed specular glint)
Magnetic Spike Alone YELLOW Metal object, worth a lens check (Ferrous metal anomaly)
Infrared Glow Alone (if device supported) YELLOW IR glow detected (Worth a physical inspection)
Clean Baseline (No signals above threshold) GREEN Clear (Absence of signal; never says "Safe")
The Four Inviolable Verdict Rules
  1. Rule 1: Strong Glint Alone CAN Reach RED. Cheap micro-cameras housed in plastic casings have almost no magnetic signature. If magnetic confirmation were mandatory, the app would be blind to plastic spy cameras.
  2. Rule 2: Magnetic Spike Alone Can NEVER Reach RED. Hotel walls are full of harmless screws, hinges, and studs. Treating metal alone as a camera would cause endless false alarms and destroy credibility.
  3. Rule 3: Brief Glint + Metal Escalates to RED. Two independent physical anomalies occurring at the same spot corroborate each other.
  4. Rule 4: GREEN Means "Clear", Never "Safe". Absence of detection is not proof of absence. The word "safe" is programmatically banned.

9.2 Judge Defense Matrix

Q1: "The magnetometer can't distinguish a camera from a screw."

Answer: "Correct, and we explicitly declare that in our UI and code. The magnetometer is a ferrous metal pre-filter, not a camera detector. In verdict.js, a magnetic spike is strictly prohibited from triggering a RED verdict on its own. It guides where to point the lens sweep, but the optical lens glint is the primary detector."

Q2: "Why don't you publish an accuracy percentage like 98%?"

Answer: "Because publishing an invented accuracy score without clinical testing across thousands of physical environments is mathematically dishonest. A user who trusts a '98% safe' badge and gets a false negative is worse off. That is why we provide raw physical metrics and let the user inspect the evidence."

Q3: "What about a plastic hidden camera that contains no metal?"

Answer: "A cheap pinhole camera housed in plastic has virtually zero magnetic signature. That is precisely why our Rule 1 allows a strong persistent glint alone to trigger a RED verdict. If we had required magnetic confirmation, the app would be blind to the most common cheap spy cameras."

Q4: "Your scan runs at 2–3 FPS. How would you productionize this?"

Answer: "Expo Go does not expose camera frame processor callbacks, so we capture downscaled JPEG stills in JS for hackathon compatibility. To productionize, we swap in react-native-vision-camera with C++ frame processors running at 30 FPS on the camera thread. Every mathematical function in src/logic/ is 100% pure and survives that transition completely unchanged."

Q5: "Did you actually build this logic or generate it?"

Answer: "Run npm run selftest on our laptop. 33 assertions execute in 0.8 seconds against synthetic camera frames and magnetic traces, mathematically verifying blob rejection, shape filtering, temporal persistence, and all verdict rules."
